# Character mechanics (src/game/mechanics)

The rules the client computes itself for the local player: effect totals (TotalEffect), stats, resists and caps,
maximum and current HP / mana / endurance, armor class, haste, weight, regeneration, skills and skill caps, buff
durations and cast time. They run on plain data (`CharacterSheet`) and are covered by recorded test vectors
(`tests/*_vectors_test.cpp`).

## Exactness

- Integer math wraps (explicit unsigned adds and multiplies).
- x87: the precision control is 24-bit in game, because the graphics layer creates its Direct3D 9 device without
  `D3DCREATE_FPU_PRESERVE` (behaviour flags 0x24/0x84/0x44 | 4). `Fpu` rounds every x87 result exactly as the FPU
  does at either precision (TwoSum / fma error terms decide the double-rounding ties); `-ffp-contract=off` keeps
  the compiler from fusing operations. Example: drunkenness 64 and INT 150 give 102 at 24 bits and 101 at 53 bits.
- `tests/mechanics_vectors_test.cpp` checks every vector record (callees mocked; the hooks replay those callees), at
  each record's precision.
- A deliberate difference where the original never ends: shadow step (SPA 379) walks its distance down by 2 as a
  float while it is above 5; from 2^25 up `distance - 2` can round back to `distance`, and the original's loop runs
  for ever on that spell. Hailstone stops at the first step that leaves the distance unchanged (the loop depends on
  the distance alone, so every base that ends in the original ends at the same step here).

Vectors without a mocked-callee list (movement animation selection, old-style animation speed) are checked in
`tests/anim_vectors_test.cpp`. The hit-by-spell vectors (`tests/hit_by_spell_vectors_test.cpp`) record every mocked
callee with its arguments and answer and the fake virtuals' calls, but not the fakes' answers: the test ports the
generator (its xorshift and world builder) to rebuild each record's world, checks the rebuilt inputs against the
record, then replays the port against the recorded calls and compares every output. Texts passed from a callee's frame
are compared as the frame holds them at the end; byte arguments pushed as dwords compare by their low byte.

The cast-spell vectors (`tests/cast_spell_vectors_test.cpp`: checks 1000, buffs 600, items 1000, target 1000, range
1200, body type 1200, send 800) work the same way: the records leave out the player distance answer and whether the
position-change fake took effect, so the test ports the generator's randomizer and its stream (seeds 0xca51..0xca57),
checks the rebuilt inputs against each record, and replays the port call by call. It compares the return value, the
target, the caster fields written (sentinels, as the generator), the request counters and the payload, and checks
that `net::encode(cast_spell_packet(...))` gives the OP_CastSpell bytes (+0x1d..+0x1f and an unset position left out,
as the records do).

The use-skill vectors (`tests/use_skill_vectors_test.cpp`: combat, activity, target, gather, 1500 each) replay
`out.calls` strictly (each callee's name, arguments and place among the fake virtuals' notes; a null argument is a
byte never written and passes; an `xx` packet byte must be 0 from the port), then compare `out.me` and `out.globals`.
The player's own name is not among the inputs: when the target is the player the checker takes it from the
`trim_name` or `chat_template` call. In each family only that family's mocks are installed, so a record can show an
unmocked callee's inner calls; the port's queries are named after what the record lists.

The melee vectors (`tests/melee_vectors_test.cpp`: melee range 3000, attack speed 4000 with the bag search replayed
from `out.calls`, hand-to-hand delay 3000, double attack 4000, dual wield 4000 with Random() from `in.rand`)
compare the result bit for bit. Melee range's NaN results are the positive quiet NaN (0x7fc00000), which the
exporter writes as `"nan"` (`tools/re/export_vectors.py`) rather than the x87 default 0xffc00000; `vector_files.h`
reads it so. The attack (1200 records alternating x87 24- and 53-bit precision) is replayed call by call: every
callee is an `AttackQueries` call, `RecordedAttack` takes the next record of `out.calls`, checks its name and
arguments (can-I-hit's range as float bits, the combat ability attack's target, item, slot and skill, the weapon
lookups' slot and fist flag, the held models) and returns its result; items are the vectors' numbers (0..11, -2 the
fist, -1 none) passed as opaque `ItemRef`s. Then the timers, the combat skill timers and delays and the fist's
definition are compared. The player distance is the record's distance plus distance_extra at the precision. The x87
range sum (fild / fadd) is at the precision too, though the vectors' integer ranges never make it differ from a
double sum. The vectors' item field called "delay" is the item's skill: it is the Delay byte (item packet, live
cadence), which the fist and any hand-to-hand (type 0x16) item use as a skill for the reuse time. The attack's
timing and the swing timers also have unit tests there (the first swing a whole delay after attack on, too early: no
swing and the timer kept, last + delay, the restart after two delays, the refusals' messages, the fist / secondary /
ranged delays).

## Not covered by vectors

Item sums in process_all_stats (`item_sums.*`), process-affects, the mana regen tick and the endurance upkeep
(`ticks.*`) are checked against vectors.

Not checked against the original's vectors yet: the buff-slot and worn-item effect caches, the recommended-level
scaling, the is-spellcaster check and the focus level falloff percent. The AA focus collector's iterator is modelled (an
AA focus effect takes the limits that follow it).

The distance modifier (CharacterZoneClient::CalcAffectChange @0x436670, PlayerClient::CalcSpellDistanceMod
@0x59fbe0; no recorded vectors; unit tests in `tests/mechanics_test.cpp`): a spell with a distance range
(columns 227..230: near distance and modifier, far distance and modifier, and the parser's ratio +0x4e9) scales the
generic value by the modifier at the spawn's 2D distance from the player (clamped to the range, at least 0; the far
modifier when the spawn is not found), rounded half away from zero, a negative value rounding to 0 giving -1. The
buff cache measures from the slot's caster (`SheetBuff::caster_distance`, filled by the sheet builder from the
spawn list), HitBySpell from its caster, FindAffectSlot from the existing buff's caster; worn effects and the base2
values are not scaled. The player's extra long-buff slots (vtable +0x2c, @0x580460) are one from level 72 and two
from 75 (`affect::pc_extra_buff_slots`).

The spell cache's buff value for SPA 57 is `cached_buff(57, k)` (the buff slot effects key SPA 57 by Base2), which
the levitation mode reads; the spell descriptions (`game/spell_description.h`) call
`calc_affect_change(spell, level, slot, nullptr, Base, true)` (no target, so no distance scaling) and
`spell_duration`.

## Frame rate: the game clock

The original runs its game rules once per drawn frame, and several are written per frame: the air's friction (0.99
a frame) against gravity scaled by time, the turn ramp (one step a frame up to 12), LookSpring, /follow's turn.
Run as written, they depend on the frame rate: a fall at 500 frames a second never lands hard enough for fall damage
or the landing sound, a running jump carries a third as far, and at 15 frames a second falls hit far harder.

Hailstone (a deliberate difference, `hailstone.ini` `game.fixed_step`, on by default) runs them as the original
would at a steady 60 frames a second, whatever it draws at (`core/step_clock.h`). A drawn frame runs the steps that
came due since the last one: none at high frame rates, several below 60. A step's time is what the original's
millisecond clock read at 60 frames a second (steps 16, 17 and 17 ms apart). A frame longer than 15 steps (250 ms:
a hitch, a zone's load) runs 15 and drops the rest, so the game slows for that frame instead of running a burst.
Whether the original itself was played at 60 is not known; 60 is the rate chosen to match.

On the game clock (app/client_app_player.cpp, `update_player`'s steps):

- The player's movement: ProcessControls (the keys, the turn ramp, LookSpring), the physics, the collision pass, the
  landing check and fall damage, the position samples (OP_FloatListThing), the helm of a boat.
- /follow's and /nav's steering.
- Other spawns' and the boats' physics between updates (AdvanceSpawn's per-step friction and its 3-tick cap; the
  landing on the floor), `WorldState::physics_steps`. Their drawn place eases over the drawn frame as before.
- Missiles (`MissileList::update` at each step's time: a missile moves every other step, its 32 ms gate).
- The weather: ApplyWeatherState's fog band (a fixed 4 or 6 an update), the strikes, and the drops' count (round(count
  x step x p) per step; the drops move by the drawn frame, `PrecipitationField::Frame::motion_seconds`).
- CDisplay_UpdateSkyWeatherAndFog's steps: the player's light level (0.01 a frame) and the fog (`SceneFogInput::steps`;
  a frame without a step shows the state unmoved).
- The timers that run once a frame and wait for a frame past their interval, at each step's time
  (`GameConnection::set_game_steps`): scribing (3 of 92 ticks a frame), memorizing, the 1 s and 6 s ticks, the socials'
  lines and `/pause`.

Not on it: what is timed already (casting, cool-downs, doors and lifts, the network, animations, particles, sounds),
and the scripts' and plugins' frame (`mq.delay`, OnPulse), which runs once a drawn frame as in MacroQuest.

Once a drawn frame, as before: the keys and clicks (a press is kept for the next step), the mouse look (it turns the
heading directly), the camera. The camera is drawn between the last two steps (a share of the last step's move and
turn behind), so it moves smoothly at any refresh rate, at most one step (17 ms) behind. The player's body, and the
mount it rides, is drawn at that same place and heading (`WorldState::set_local_drawn`), not eased toward it as other
spawns are: eased (a twelfth of a second), it trailed the camera behind the player. Movement events (the jump, the landing sound, fall damage) of all the
frame's steps are gathered for its sounds.

`game.fixed_step = 0` runs one step per drawn frame at its own time: the original's code as written, for comparing.
`tests/frame_rate_test.cpp` plays a fall, a running jump, a held turn, a running and a coasting NPC and a scribe at 15
to 1000 drawn frames a second and checks they come out the same.

## Movement inputs

`PlayerMovement::update` (app/player_movement.cpp) takes the character's side of a movement frame from
`movement_facts(const Character&)` (`game/mechanics/movement_inputs.h`), the same Character StatsUi builds each frame
from the profile, the inventory and the buffs (`process_all_stats()` has run). Before the first Character exists a
fallback with approximations is used (the buffs' base values per SPA, the profile's STR / STA and raw skills, heroic
AGI 0, plain item weights). Each value, with what produces it:

| Input | Source |
|---|---|
| run rate | `TotalEffect(99)` and `TotalEffect(3)` (items, AAs and buffs) through `movement_rate_effect`; the running floor `TotalEffect(271)`; the speed cap raise `TotalEffect(290)` |
| swim speed | adjusted skill 50 |
| weight factor | `calculate_weight`: current weight = carried weight (`item_weight`: each item's own weight, charm-scaled, without its augments or its stack count; the bag reduction, SPA 221), capacity = STR with items and buffs, current / max HP with the spawn type, and `slowdown_any_level` (the level threshold 0 instead of 3); bazaar and barter zones from the zone table |
| fall damage | current weight, adjusted skill 39, the profile's Safe Fall skill, `TotalEffect(228)`, the heroic AGI bonus; the no-fall-damage flag is the zone's (OP_NewZone +894) |
| air supply and region damage | max air supply with STA with buffs and `TotalEffect(246)`; water breathing `TotalEffect(14)`; the fire / cold (disease in zone 200) resists from process_all_stats |
| fly mode | `local_fly_mode` (below): the spawn record and OP_SpawnAppearance 19 set it, a levitate buff landing or fading sets it, and `cached_buff(57, 0 / 1)` recomputes it at each effect cache rebuild |
| sneaking | the Sneak flag, set by the server's OP_SpawnAppearance type 15 and cleared by OP_CancelSneakHide or a cast's make-me-visible; the control processing then uses `sneak_speed(class, level)` x 0.46 forward and halves the walk back speed (`controls.cpp`) |

The local player's fly mode (the physics object's +0xc) is state, as in the original, with several writers
(`mech::local_fly_mode`, applied in this order within a frame):

- a levitate buff fading with no levitation left (`CharacterZoneClient_RemoveAffect` @0x439700, SPA 57 case,
  `PlayerZoneClient::Levitating` @0x5a1140) sets 0, a flying mode 1 included;
- a levitate buff landing (`EQ_Character::HitBySpell`'s SPA 57, FUN_00432b20 @0x432b20) sets 2, or 5 for a
  non-zero Base2, over mode 1 too; 0 in a no-levitation zone;
- the server sets any mode 0 to 5 outright, with no rule applied: the spawn record at zone-in (UnSerializeSpawn) and
  OP_SpawnAppearance 19 (`Handle_OP_SpawnAppearance` @0x4b4b60, `PlayerPhysics_SetFlyMode` @0x8d0910; a value above 5
  is ignored, the original throws);
- the effect cache rebuild (`ReCacheSpellEffects` @0x438910, ending in `UpdateLevitationState` @0x433480) gives 2 or
  5 from the levitate buffs, else 0; it keeps mode 1, a rider takes its mount's mode, and a no-levitation zone forces 0.

The rebuild runs only when the cache is cleared, so a server-set mode lasts until the buffs next change: after GM
`#set flymode 3` the next buff landing or fading puts the player back on the ground (or levitating), and
`#set flymode 0` while levitating lasts until then too. `#set flymode 1` survives buff changes, except a levitate buff
landing or fading, and in a no-levitation zone the next rebuild grounds it. Hailstone sees the buff slots rather than
the packets that clear the cache: any slot whose spell changes is a rebuild, a slot that loses a levitate spell is a
fade and one that gains one is a landing (docs/unknowns.md). Zoning in applies the spawn record and then rebuilds.
Tests: `tests/movement_test.cpp` ("Local fly mode", "Buff levitation change") and `tests/fly_mode_test.cpp` (through
the connection's packets).

Tests: `tests/movement_inputs_test.cpp` builds synthetic characters (buffs, worn items, augments, bags, heroic gear)
and checks each value against the hand-worked result; the ports themselves keep their vectors
(`tests/movement_vectors_test.cpp`, `tests/mechanics_vectors_test.cpp`).

Live check (2026-10-25, `--move-test speed`, a level-60 cleric with a server-granted Echo of Selo, SPA 3
10 + 2 x level = 130): SpeedRun = RunSpeed x weight + the modifier (0.7 x 1 + 0.91 = 1.61) gives 68.4 units/s
measured (42.67 x 1.61 = 68.7 expected), walking 0.3 + 0.91 = 1.21 gives 51.6/s; at 48 of 2755 HP the weight
factor is 0.5 (SpeedRun 1.26) and at 87 HP 0.75 (1.435). The server agrees: EQEmu's run speed for it is
base 28 (0.7 x 40) + 130 % = 64, i.e. 1.6 x 40; its MovementCheck compares the distance x 100 / ms (6.9 here)
with that number, so it never flags a client at these speeds, it logged no POSSIBLE_HACK, and the position it
saved on disconnect was the client's to the last digit (no rubber-banding).

## Movement and view keys

`__ExecuteCmd`'s movement commands go to `execute_movement_command` (game/mechanics/controls.h) on each press and
release, and ProcessControls reads what they hold: the move, turn and strafe keys (0..7), the look keys PITCHUP /
PITCHDOWN (17 / 18: CameraAngle ramps by count x rate twice a step, count up to 12, clamped to +-128), CENTERVIEW
(19, below), the zoom keys (20 / 21: the active camera's distance by rate x 2 a step, not nearer than the spawn's
AvatarHeight nor farther than 400) and the camera keys (328..333). The rate is g_LocalPhysicsTimeFactor, the last
physics step's elapsed ms x 0.02 (1/3 at 60 steps a second). The look, zoom and camera keys work while frozen; the
move keys and CENTERVIEW do not. During mouse look (the right button held, or MOUSELOOK_ENGAGE) the turn keys strafe
in the first person, overhead and chase cameras and the camera behind the player.

HOT1_1 .. HOT10_12 press bar (command - 37) / 12's buttons and HOTPAGE1_1 .. HOTPAGE10_10 turn bar
(command - 157) / 10's page.

## Mouse buttons as keys

The middle button and the buttons past right (DirectInput's 2..7: middle, X1, X2 and three more) are keys. Each
frame, at the end of __ProcessMouseEvents, 0x51a770 compares them with the last frame and sends a press as key
code 0xf0 + n through HandleKeyDownEvent (the held Shift / Ctrl / Alt join it, as for a key) and a release through
HandleKeyUpEvent, so they bind like any key and show as "MOUS MID" and "MOUS AUX1".."MOUS AUX5". 0x51a770
itself checks for no window under the cursor. Hailstone passes them to the key map with the keys, so whatever holds
the keys back (an edit box, a modal dialog, the loading screen) holds them back too. The left and right buttons are
never keys. Hailstone reads SDL's buttons 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 as these
(`platform::dik_from_sdl_mouse_button`). A mouse whose extra buttons its own software sends as keyboard keys was
already bindable through those keys.

## Cameras

`game/cameras.h` ports the original's cameras (EverQuest::Cameras, CDisplay::cameraType): first person (0), overhead
(1), chase (2), user 1 and 2 (3, 4) and tether (5), plus the wheel-follow camera (6), which is this client's own camera
behind the player (its distance the mouse wheel's, the gamepad's stick its own). It stops against scenery as the
cameras 1..5 do, where the 0.25 sphere swept from the eye stops (`wheel_follow_position`); it used to step its
distance down by a fifth until the line behind was clear, which in a small room jumped between the steps as the
player walked. The player's model is drawn in every camera but first person.

| Camera | Where it stands (each frame, on the player as drawn) | Mouse look: sideways / up-down | Keys that act |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overhead (1) | above the eye by the height (25), looking the player's way at the pitch (-100) | the player turns / the pitch (+-128) | pan (height), pitch |
| Chase (2) | behind the player's heading by eye height + distance, above the eye by the height; looks at the player, 8.5 below its pitch (all 0 by default) | the player turns / the height | all |
| User 1, 2 (3, 4) | around the player at their own heading and distance (30 / 192 / height 5; 82 / 277 / 18), looking at the player's eye | the camera turns / the distance (at least 3) | all |
| Tether (5) | keeps last frame's direction from the player and is pulled along at 4..20 units; the pitch raises it; the turn keys turn it once | the camera turns / the pitch (-120..64) | pan, pitch |

The distance is clamped to 2..200 (overhead and chase: at most 200) and the height to 0..30. Each camera stands where a
0.25 sphere swept from the player's eye toward the point stops (unk_EQCamera_SetPosition, against the zone and its
objects). TOGGLECAM (CDisplay::ToggleView, also the hot button's) cycles 0 -> 1 -> .. -> 5 -> 0, and from the camera
behind the player to the overhead; FIRST_PERSON_CAMERA .. TETHER_CAMERA pick one. The mouse wheel zooms only between
first person and the camera behind the player; any other camera puts that camera's distance to 0. The zoom and camera
turn keys act only in the chase and user cameras (their virtual +0x2c); the pan and pitch keys in any but first person
(the camera behind the player takes neither: a deliberate difference, it is this client's). CENTERVIEW levels the
pitch in first person, sets the overhead's back to -100 and points the others at the player again (+0x44); a pitch
key or the mouse's pitch on a user camera stops that. The gamepad's camera button switches between first person and the
camera behind the player, as before; its stick turns the player in the cameras 1..5.

Deliberate differences: the cameras keep the client's own field of view (ToggleView gives each camera's +0x40, 90 by
default); the user cameras are not saved (the original's loadOptions reads Camera2-* into camera 1's globals, so its
zoom check always fails and both are reset to their defaults each session: the same here); zoning does not restore
the saved camera. Knockout selects overhead until consciousness returns; a drunkenness tick at 70 or above selects
first person and widens the FOV by the value above 70. The widening is added on top of the player's own FOV
(Options, /fov) instead of replacing it with camera 0's base FOV, and drops at once when drunkenness falls below 70
(a reset at death or bind too), so the chosen FOV comes back exactly. The missile camera
(8, following the player's own arrow) is not used.

## Movement collision

How a move meets walls:

- `PlayerMovement::update` (app/player_movement.cpp) runs the physics Step, then the landing check (inside Step),
  then `do_collision_movement` (CollisionPass) from the actor's location to the integrated position. The spawn's z
  is the feet plus AvatarHeight (3.125 x size x 0.2 for the player races).
- A placement (the zone-in, a teleport, a height change, a test placement) finds its floor first, from the placed
  spawn z, before the physics Step: the floor height at the OP_ZoneEntry position, and the teleport's player floor
  height. The first landing check then stands the feet on it. A zone point's z is often the ground itself (freportw

  colliding. The jump animation is the animation object's vf24 on +0x10c0 (`is_jump_animation`: L03 / L04 on
  classic models, JMPA / JMPU on new-style ones), as the renderer last chose it for the controlled spawn; a spawn
  not drawn (first person) counts the 500 ms after a jump instead.
- Held in the air (Hailstone, a deliberate difference, `PlayerMovement::update`): when the collision movement keeps
  the player where it was without being stuck (a still move under 0.01, or one that barely moved), it zeroes SpeedZ.
  Over a floor that stops a still player; in the air, above about 126 frames a second, gravity alone cannot make a
  move of 0.01 in one frame (0.4 x dt^2, dt in 50 ms ticks), so each hold zeroed the speed again and a jump hung at
  its top until a slow frame came. A body on the ground fly mode, dry and more than 0.05 over its floor keeps its

  ran at and the body falls on. At 60 frames a second at most one frame of a jump differs. On the game clock ("Frame rate") a step is never

Ladders (`game/zone_ladders.*`, `ladder.*`):

- A ladder is an actor of type 6: EQObject_Construct gives that type to an object whose actor tag starts with
  LADDER. Besides ground objects, about thirty classic zones place them as static objects in objects.wld
  (LADDER14 .. LADDER60, LADDERCB101, LADDERDK400 ...: Qeynos, Guk, Unrest, Blackburrow, Ak'Anon, Cabilis ...).
  Most of their meshes are solid (LADDER14 .. LADDER60: thin slats 0.7 deep); every polygon of Cabilis's LADDERCB101
  (the canal and tower ladders, 13 in Cabilis East) has the WLD passable flag.
- Walking into one: the movement sweep's hit callback (CollisionHit_SetCollidingType) writes the actor's type to the
  player's CollidingType, so a kept hit on a ladder makes it 6. DoCollisionMovement zeroes it on every moving step,
  so it stays 6 only while the player keeps meeting the ladder. At 6, GetEnvCoefficients returns the ladder set
  (0.8, 0.8, 0.625, 0.625) before gravity, and the run push's vertical part (sin(pitch) x SpeedRun) is kept: forward
  while looking up climbs, looking down goes down. The zone's ladder triangles stay in the static mesh marked
  `kTriangleLadder`; the sweep's final mesh triangle being one counts as the ladder's kept hit, tested before the
  doors (the original's order among the actors is not traced).
- A click on one runs Ladder_MountClickedLadder: from the top (feet above 0.7 of its height), within 25, the
  player is put on it facing down. A zone ladder's heading is its placement's first angle; its height is the top of
  its bounds at its scale, passable triangles included (the actor's vtable +0x60 is not traced). The pick's first
  pass sets passableBlocking, so a passable ladder is clicked like a solid one.
- A passable ladder (LADDERCB101) in the original: the DLL's model sweep (0x100c5af0) skips a passable triangle unless
  its flags also have bit 2, in which case a hit short of the stop runs the hit callback without stopping the sphere.
  The WLD model loader (0x10057540) sets bit 0 alone, and nothing seen sets bit 2, so the sphere goes through the
  ladder, never climbs it, and the canal ladders are left only by a click from the top (going down). Hailstone's own
  rule, on by default and switched by the server's `hailstone.cfg` `[movement] passable_ladders`
  (docs/plugins.md): after the movement sweep, a passable ladder triangle the sphere meets short of its stop, or
  overlaps where the move starts, counts as the ladder's hit (`CollisionMesh::touches_passable_ladder`). The
  sphere still goes through it, and the climb then runs as for a solid ladder; swimming at a canal ladder from the
  canal, looking up, reaches the street.
- Solid ladders (Hailstone's own rule, on by default and switched by `hailstone.cfg` `[movement] solid_ladders`): the
  player's movement meets a solid ladder as one slab, the box of its solid polygons placed as the ladder is (so it
  leans with it), instead of its rails and rungs (`game::ladder_slab`, `CollisionMesh::set_solid_ladders`). The
  movement sweep, the floor test and CanIFitHere's ceiling all see the slab; sight, picking, rain and the navigation
  mesh still see the rungs. Without it, with the stand-in sweep radius (0.475 x AvatarHeight, "Movement collision"),
  the rung ladders could not be climbed reliably by any race. A small race's sphere (a gnome's 0.89) goes between
  the rungs (3.2 apart) or off the ladder's edge, stops meeting the ladder and falls. And CanIFitHere takes the rung
  below as the floor and the rung above as the ceiling, 3.7 apart. That is less than the 2 x AvatarHeight it needs
  for every race (3.75 for a gnome), so it refuses the move and the climber sticks partway up. Upper and Lower Guk,
  Unrest and Ak'Anon each had ladders that some races (and in Ak'Anon every race) could not climb.

## NPC collision

Hailstone's own rule, a deliberate difference from the original (`game/npc_collision.h`). In RoF2 no spawn's body
stops the player (above, "Other spawns"), so players walk through NPCs. Here a live NPC stops the player:

- **Who blocks**: an NPC that is not a corpse, has no pet owner, is not a swarm pet, is not a mercenary, is not
  ridden (a mount) and is not a vehicle race (a boat's hull is its own collision, below). Players, pets of anyone,
  swarm pets, mercenaries, corpses and mounts never block.
- **Swarm pets** (`is_swarm_pet`: swarms, Wake the Dead, animated weapons): stock EQEmu sends them with no pet
  owner, so they are told by what its `NPC::PetOnSpawn` sets for a player's swarm pet: not cyclable
  (`targetable_with_hotkey` off), or the last name "<owner>'s Pet" (the default `Pets:ClientPetsUseOwnerNameInLastName`).
  An ordinary NPC the server keeps off the target-cycling keys is therefore passable too. An NPC the renderer does not draw (invisible to the player, past the clip plane) does not either.
- **The body**: a 12-sided upright prism round the spawn, of the original's NPC collision volume radius
  (`collision_volume_radius`: AvatarHeight x 0.5 x 0.35, 0.66 for a human), from 2 under its feet to twice its
  AvatarHeight above them (at least 8, so a small NPC is not stepped over). Walls only, in the movement sweep only
  (`ZoneMoveWorld::sweep`): it is no floor and no ceiling, so the player never stands on an NPC, and the floor,
  ceiling and room tests do not see it. The player's sweep stops at it and slides along it as along any wall.
- **Never held**: a sweep that starts inside a body (closer to its axis than both radii less 0.25: the NPC walked
  or spawned onto the player) ignores it, so the player walks out in any direction.
- **The switch**: the server's `hailstone.cfg` `[movement] npc_collision` (docs/plugins.md), on by default and without
  the file; off brings back the original's walk-through. The player has no switch of their own. The server moves its
  NPCs without knowing of this rule, so one can still walk into or through the player.

## Boats

A deliberate design, not a port: the original's vehicle code is not followed (only `vehicle.*`'s frame conversion is
used). What is kept is what EQEmu sends and expects. The code is `game/boats.*` with its parts in
`app/player_movement.cpp` (the carry), `app/client_app_player.cpp` (the hull, the zone-in hold, climbing aboard, the
helm) and `net/game_connection_vehicle.cpp` (the helm's packets); tests in `tests/boats_test.cpp`.

- What EQEmu does: a boat is an NPC of a vehicle race moving along its grid. It sends a position update when it
  starts, turns or changes speed and every 5 s, with the speed in the animation field and no deltas; it never fixes
  a boat's z. A rider's OP_ClientUpdate with a vehicle id is taken as a position in the boat's frame and the server
  adds the boat's position and heading back (`Handle_OP_ClientUpdate`; updates are ignored while the boat turns).
  It never sends a vehicle id back: other riders arrive as world positions. Boats change zone by quest scripts
  (`quest::movepc` from a proximity on the boat, PEQ's), which put the player at a fixed point in the next zone,
  usually its dock; nothing about the boat goes with them.
- The boat's motion: stepped between updates like other spawns (along its heading at its SpeedRun; at EQEmu's
  0.58 x speed units a second this matched the server to a fraction of a unit per update live). An update does not
  snap or ease the drawn boat: the difference becomes a display error that fades out (1.2 per second), so the deck
  never jumps and never trails behind; a jump over 60 units (a grid's return to its start) is a teleport. Vehicles
  are stepped before the player moves (`WorldState::smooth_vehicles`), so the player is carried by, and collides
  with, the boat where it is drawn this frame.
- Standing on it: the floor test that finds the deck sets the vehicle (+0x150). Each frame the body, its velocity,
  heading and last good position go from the boat's last drawn pose to its new one, so the player keeps its place
  and facing on a moving, turning deck, and walks and jumps on it relative to the deck. The position goes out in the
  drawn boat's frame (the deck the player stands on, not the server's estimate).
- The hull: the model's triangles are taken in the boat's frame from the drawn model (skinned at its animation's
  current frame) and taken again every 200 ms, so whatever the model's own animation moves collides where it was
  drawn a moment ago; between takes they are placed at the drawn pose each frame. They are floors for the floor test
  and walls for the movement sweeps, so rails stop the player and the hull keeps swimmers out.
- Overlapping vehicles: the floor test stands the player on the vehicle whose triangle stopped the sweep nearest (a
  higher deck over a lower one); two decks level to 0.01 units go to the vehicle the player already stands on, so the
  carry does not swap between them from frame to frame (`game::sweep_hulls`).
- Rails: while the vehicle carrying the player moves, a walking step that would leave its deck over a floor more than
  4 units below (the sea) is undone and the player stays where the deck carried it. Flat decks such as the LAUNCH
  skiff's have no rails in their models and slide out from under a player near the edge. A jump, swimming or
  levitating goes over; a dock at the deck's height is stepped onto, and a still boat has no rails at all.
- Getting on and off: walking or jumping from a dock onto a deck is the floor test finding the deck; stepping off
  lets go. Swimming forward against a hull whose deck is at most 30 units above the feet, just past the hull, puts
  the player on the deck (a climb; there are no ladders on the models).
- Zoning: after a zone-in, while a vehicle within 80 units has no collision model yet (it is drawn a frame or two
  after it spawns), the player is held where the zone-in put it, for up to 5 s, so a drop onto a deck does not go
  through it into the sea. Where a boat script puts the player is the server's choice.
- Other spawns: one whose update lands it on a deck (a short drop test onto the decks) rides that boat: its place
  and heading in the boat's frame are kept and stepped with its own deltas (which riders send, and EQEmu passes on,
  in the boat's frame) until an update puts it elsewhere.
- The helm (OP_ControlBoat; EQEmu grants it for its controllable races 141 BOAT and 502 ROW, and echoes the
  packet): right-clicking the small boat you stand on asks for it. At the helm the movement keys sail the boat
  (14 units a second ahead at 7 per second each second, 5 astern, 40 heading units a second turning, drifting to a
  stop at 3 per second with no key) and the player stands still on the deck. A step whose waterline is out of the
  water, or that runs the hull into the zone (a dock, the shore's walls), a door or object, or another vehicle's hull,
  is refused and stops the boat; the turn alone is still tried, so the bow can swing away, and it can back off. The
  hull is 16 probes (spheres of radius 1 just inside the model's outline, at the outermost point each way) swept from
  the old pose to the new; a probe touching something where it starts is not held by it. Its position goes out as OP_ClientUpdate under the boat's id ten times a second while it moves;
  EQEmu moves the boat there and passes it on. Leaving the deck lets go.
- EQEmu's warp detector measures the rider's world position, so a boat faster than the rider's run speed is logged
  as a warp for a player below the exempt status; it only logs.

## Kept items: Hailstone's Keep mark

A deliberate difference, with no counterpart in the original: the Advanced Inventory window (Hailstone's own,
`InventoryManagerWnd`, `ui/modern/inventorymanager.lua`; the EQ menu's row after Inventory and the inventory window's
button) marks items Keep or Sell, by item id, so every copy of an item has the mark. Sell is the marks the merchant
window's Sell marked sells (`eq.SellJunk`; called junk in the code and the settings). Keep is the client's own
(`game/kept_items.h`, `GameConnection::set_item_kept`), kept per character in `kept_items.txt` in the character's
folder. While an item is kept, or a bag holds one, the client refuses, with a line in the chat saying why and the item
left where it is:

- destroying it (the inventory window's Destroy, before its confirmation, and `destroy_cursor`);
- dropping it on the ground (`request_drop_cursor`, before CanDrop and its confirmation, and `drop_cursor_on_ground`);
- putting it in a trade, with a player or an NPC (`trade_drop_cursor`, which a give to an NPC goes through);
- selling it to a merchant, a point merchant or an adventure merchant (`merchant_sell`, `point_merchant_sell`,
  `adventure_merchant_sell`); Sell marked leaves kept items out;
- sending it by parcel (`send_parcel`);
- selling to a barter buyer, posting a bazaar offer or offering it as buyer compensation;
- donating it to a personal or guild tribute master.

Nothing goes to the server: the refusals are the client not sending the move, so a stock EQEmu needs nothing.
Checks run again at confirmation, not only when a quantity dialog opens. Marking an offered item Keep ends the
active trader or buyer session; it cannot remain available for a later remote sale. A satchel item that is not listed
(no price) does not end it. An item is marked Keep or Sell,
not both: marking one clears the other. `eq.SetItemsKept` validates a batch before applying it, then persists it and
changes the Keep revision once.

## The bank as last seen

A Hailstone addition, with no counterpart in the original. EQEmu's `Inventory:LazyLoadBank` rule (on by default)
leaves the bank and shared bank out of OP_CharInventory. It sends them as item packets (type 0x67), once per zone and
only when a banker comes near the player (Client::Process, Mob::IsCloseToBanker). So after most zone-ins the client
does not know what the bank holds, and the Advanced Inventory and `/finditem` would find nothing there.

The client keeps a copy (`game/bank_snapshot.h`, `GameConnection::bank_seen`). It is taken from the inventory whenever
the bank is known and the client is about to lose it or the player has finished with it: the next OP_CharInventory,
the bank window closing, camping out and disconnecting. It is saved per character in `bank.dat` and read on entering
the world. The bank counts as known (`bank_live`) from an OP_CharInventory that holds bank items, from an item packet
for a bank or shared bank slot, or from the bank window opening. It counts as not known from an OP_CharInventory
without any.

While the bank is not known, `eq.AllItems` and `eq.FindItems` list the copy in the bank's place. Its rows are marked
`stale`, and `eq.BankSeen` says how long ago it was taken. The window shows them dimmed, says "Bank as last seen ..."
in its footer, and refuses to move or use them. The copy is never put in the inventory, so no click, bulk job, lore
check, item count or hot button can reach what it holds. Moving or using a banked item still needs the bank window
open, which needs a banker in reach. The copy may be out of date: for one, the shared bank can be changed by the
account's other characters. Opening the bank shows what the server has.

## The Advanced Inventory's bulk actions

Also Hailstone's own: selected rows in either the Advanced Inventory list or grid can be sold, donated as tribute,
destroyed or withdrawn from the bank (`GameConnection::start_bulk_job`, `eq.BulkItems`). Each runs as a job, one
item at a time, through the same packets a player's clicks would send. Sell and tribute take only carried items
(the general slots and their bags); destroy can also take banked items while the bank is open. These three actions
exclude kept items. Withdraw only moves banked items into inventory, so Keep does not block it. Nothing worn is
taken. The selection records each item's serial and quantity: a replacement in the same slot is not acted on.

- Sell, at an open merchant: each sale is OP_ShopPlayerSell for the whole stack, the next after the merchant's answer,
  as Sell marked does. Not a bag, nor what the merchant refuses (`game::sell_refusal`).
- Tribute, at an open personal or guild tribute master: each item is picked up and donated from the cursor (OP_TributeItem,
  as the window's Donate), the next after the server's answer; one it refuses (0 favour) goes back to its slot. An item
  worth no favour or a bag is left out. Guild donations use GuildFavor and wait for the guild reply counter;
  personal donations use Favor. Changing or closing the master stops the job. No answer in 5 seconds stops it.
- Withdraw, at an open bank: each chosen bank or shared-bank item goes through the cursor into the first free general
  slot, else the first bag with room (`stow_cursor_in_packs`: auto-inventory's last steps, without its worn-slot merge
  and equip, so nothing withdrawn is equipped). Selecting
  a bag and its children moves the bag once. Closing the bank stops the job; if inventory fills, the held item is
  put back when its original slot is still free.
- Destroy: each item is picked up and destroyed from the cursor (OP_MoveItem to nowhere, as DestroyHeldItemOrMoney),
  with no confirmation per item: the window asks once, naming what goes. A bag that holds anything is left out (it
  would take its contents), and so is an item with the No Destroy flag.

The window says beforehand how many of the selection each action takes. A job stops, with a line in the chat and
what the cursor holds put away, if the items change under it (the cursor, a trade, a merchant or the quantity
window), as Sort does.

Selection identity is checked before confirmation as well as while the job runs: the item ID, serial and quantity
must still match, and a destroy rechecks No Destroy and bag contents immediately before pickup and destruction.
An empty selected bag that acquired contents is left intact. Trader Add and the buyer-start acknowledgment also
recheck Keep; a late start reply cannot publish newly protected compensation.

Item donations allow one unresolved request across personal and guild tribute. Neither reply has an instance or
transaction ID, so a batch's five-second timeout retains its pending identity and another item donation waits for
the answer, a new zone/session, the same kind of master's window being answered again, or 30 seconds. Stock EQEmu
answers in the handler or not at all (Handle_OP_TributeItem returns without a reply when the master is gone or out
of range, the guild handler when the character has no guild), so the answer to a master opened after the donation
comes after any answer to it; closing the window alone keeps waiting, so a reply still in flight is reconciled. A reply reconciles only one unchanged original instance, even if it moved;
replacement cursor items and ambiguous or changed stacks are preserved, with a message to refresh by zoning.

  nearest actor has the required bit. In a pass the world's triangles go first (back faces skipped; a hit needs

  best (the first tested wins a tie). Type 2 actors and mode 1 actors whose model definition has +0x5c are spheres

## Spawn heights

`game/spawn_heights.*` (on `fpu.h`), vectors in `tests/spawn_heights_vectors_test.cpp` (SetHeights 3000,
ComputeSpecialHeights 1800, ChangeHeight 1500, collision volume radius 2000 against `collision_volume_radius`,

- `set_heights`: Length 0; a Height not above 0 (a NaN is kept) takes racedata.txt's default (column 89, or 90 for
  gender 1; 6.0 for a missing row or a value below 1); AvatarHeight = ROffset (moddat.ini `[code] ROffset`, 3.125)
  x the scale, the scale unrounded: Height x 1/6 x 1.3 for new-style races (flag 0x08), Height x 0.2 for classic
  ones, the 1.3 left out / x 0.7692308 while the new-zone flag is set; ViewHeight = 6.2 x the stored scale;
  ComputeSpecialHeights; ViewHeight += the view offset; a special scale is stored without recomputing the others;
  JumpStrength 1.35, SwimStrength 3.375; MaxSpeakDistance AvatarHeight x 0.3 (from 4.0) or x 0.6, at least 0.3; an
  AvatarHeight of 0 becomes 0.1.
- `change_height` (OP_ChangeSize): Height = size, SetHeights(viewOffset, animScale), the actor's scale, and with an
  actor its volume radius; the mount chain the same with SetHeights(0, 1.0).
- `eye_height`, `calc_scale_factor_for_height`, `actor_height_fixup` and `place_in_world` (the fix-up table and
  in-world raise applied when a player actor is created).

How the client uses them: `WorldState` keeps each spawn's `heights` (`set_heights` on OP_ZoneEntry / OP_NewSpawn and
after an illusion, `change_height` on OP_ChangeSize, every spawn again when OP_NewZone changes the new-zone flag
(NewZone +0x2b4; 1 until the first OP_NewZone), `make_actors` for the fix-up once the actor renderer has the
spawn's model). feet = z - AvatarHeight everywhere: the actor is drawn at z (EQG models lowered by ROffset x scale,
the ROOT_BONE drop), boxes and the target ring from the feet, remote spawns kept at floor + AvatarHeight between
updates, the player's AvatarHeight for the collision sweep (its sphere radius stays `sweep_radius`'s value, see
"Movement collision"), the floor test, the fit-here test, regions (the head at z + ViewHeight - AvatarHeight) and
ladders; the camera eye is z + eye height. A change of the player's AvatarHeight keeps its z (the feet move). At
zone-in the player's position is its own OP_ZoneEntry record's, raised by 2 up to four times while outside the
world's regions, else the zone's safe point.

Remote spawns and the floor: a moving spawn on the ground (flymode 0) is kept at floor + AvatarHeight; one at rest
settles onto a floor at most 5 below or above it (`WorldState::smooth`), so an NPC that an EQEmu server with a poor
map leaves standing higher stays in the air.

Stopping: another spawn is stepped on with its last update's SpeedRun and turn rate while that update is at most 12 s
old (AdvanceSpawn). A SpawnAppearance stand state of sitting (110), looting (105) or lying down (115, feign death)
clears both (PlayerZoneClient::ChangePosition @0x5a0bc0's StopMoving), so the spawn slides to a stop on the velocity
it had instead of running on. The local player's body loses them the same way, and its SpeedRun when a stun starts
(CharacterZoneClient::StunMe @0x4432c0, the mount's too; `GameConnection::speed_stops`): ProcessControls only stops
asking for speed while the player cannot move.

Stuns (OP_Stun, the case @0x4c7093: StunMe(duration, 0, 0, the byte at +4)). StunMe does nothing under stun immunity
(TotalEffect(40) > 0) or while a stun's timer runs; a duration below 1000 ms is 1000 ms for a player, so EQEmu's
UnStun, an OP_Stun of 0 (a fear fading with Combat:EnableFearPathing off, a quest's UnStun), stuns for a second when
none runs and changes nothing when one does. A stun sets the timer and the stunned state, prints "You are stunned!"
(chat filter 0x1a hides it), stops the player and its mount, sets the listener's room 0x17, and with the byte at +4
sets the turn rate to 32, which turns the player until it can move again (EQEmu sends 0). DoPassageOfTime clears the
timer once it passes and ends the stun (EndStun: "You are unstunned.") unless the player is mesmerized; then the mez's
removal ends it.

Grounding NPCs (a deliberate difference, Hailstone's option `ground_npcs`: hailstone.ini `display.ground_npcs`,
Options > Graphics "Ground Floating NPCs", on by default): an NPC or corpse on the ground that stands more than 0.25
and at most 50 over the floor there (the zone's terrain and placed objects, and the doors, as the player's own floor
test has them: Plane of Knowledge's portal stones are doors, and an NPC on one's base stands on it) is drawn at floor +
AvatarHeight. The landing check above keeps the zone's floor alone. That covers both gaps the landing check leaves: more than 5, and any gap of a spawn whose last update is
more than 12 s old (it is no longer stepped, so it never settles). Flymode 3 counts as on the ground: EQEmu sends it
(GravityBehavior::Water) for NPCs that may swim, such as East Commonlands' spiderlings (race 440, seen live 0.93 over
the floor). Left alone: flying and levitating spawns (flymode 1 and 2, SpawnAppearance 19; EQEmu's 4 and 5 too),
riders, spawns on a boat, players, and spawns in or over water, slime or lava regions (fish, a dock the zone files
lack). Higher than 50 the server more likely means it. A spawn is measured again when it moves, when a zone's
collision loads and when a door comes, goes or moves (spawns come before it, while character select's scene is still loaded); with no floor under it, every
frame until there is one. Only `display_position` is lowered, so the model, name, target ring, picking and the NPC
walls follow the drawn body; `position`, which range, melee and the other rules use, keeps the server's z.

Riding while illusioned (a deliberate difference, Hailstone's option `illusion_mounts`: hailstone.ini
`game.illusion_mounts`, Options > General "Ride While Illusioned", on by default): the original's
PlayerClient_AttachRiderToMount (game/mounts.cpp) refuses a rider whose current model has no tunic point (0x58dde0;

## Group roles

The group leader assigns Main Tank (1), Main Assist (2) and Puller (3) by right-clicking a member's name in the
Group window, or their own name in the Player window, in either UI pack. Small role icons beside the names show
the assignments, with the full role names on hover. A member may hold several roles, while each role belongs to
at most one member; mercenaries may only hold Main Tank.
On stock EQEmu, assigning a role requires the member to be in the leader's zone. An existing role can also be
cleared while its holder is in another zone; the menus explain this restriction.

Deliberate differences from RoF2 (Hailstone's, marked in `app/lua_eq_hud.cpp` and `GameConnection::send_group_role`):

- The original's roles are a "Roles" submenu of the Group and Player windows' own context menu (added by
  CGroupWnd::sub_0067ac60 @0x67ac60 and CPlayerWnd::sub_00717f60 @0x717f60). Hailstone opens the role menu on its
  own from a right click on the name (Y on a row in the gamepad pack), and leaves the window menu to the rest of the
  frame; the menu has no "Window" row.
- The original offers Roles only to the group leader (the Group window's test: FUN_00443670 in 0x67ac60): other
  members get no Roles row at all. Hailstone shows everyone in a group the menu, with the role rows greyed for all
  but the leader.
- The original's submenu starts with "Leader" (item 100, /makeleader; built in CGroupWnd's constructor @0x67bc00).
  Hailstone's menu is roles only; /makeleader stays.
- For a mercenary the original offers every row and refuses Leader, Main Assist and Puller on the click with eqstr
  7607 "A NPC cannot be that role." (CGroupWnd::WndNotification @0x67c620). Hailstone greys those rows instead;
  `send_group_role` still refuses with 7607 for any other caller.
- The original's menu sends Send_OP_GroupRoles without checking leadership or CanUseCommandNow, relying on Roles
  being offered to the leader only. Hailstone's `send_group_role` checks both (0x1db1 for a non-leader, then
  CanUseCommandNow's refusals), as Cmd_grouproles @0x4f8360 does for `/grouproles set|unset`, so a menu left open
  after leadership changed cannot send.

The UI and `/grouproles set|unset <member> <role number>` share `GameConnection::send_group_role`, which checks
the current group, leader, member and role before sending the existing `OP_GroupRoles` packet. Sending a request
does not change local roles: `OP_GroupRoles` replies and the `OP_GroupUpdateB` roster set the flags. The UI's
icons and menu checks, and scripts' `Group.MainTank`, `Group.MainAssist`, `Group.Puller` and member role flags, read
that same state. Assigning a role clears its previous holder when the server confirms it. A group disband or
zone transition clears the old state, and the server's roster and role updates restore it.

## AFK and the NPC target keys

Actor drawing, nameplates, mouse picking and PC/NPC target cycling share viewer visibility (`game::spawn_invisible`, PlayerZoneClient::IsInvisible @0x5a1850). Spawn invisibility ranks and SpawnAppearance type 3 updates are compared with the viewer's SPA 13 total; group members have the awareness exemption, and a GM target stays hidden from a non-GM viewer whose rank reaches it. The player's own model is hidden only for a GM whose hide mode is 3001 (the function's first test). Corpses and zero-HP targets are never hidden; any other spawn with property 100 (PlayerBase::HasProperty(0x64): body type 100 or 100 in the spawn record's property list) is always hidden, whatever the viewer sees (before this, Hailstone drew such spawns). The viewer's SPA 13 total is built from the character sheet only while some spawn is hidden at a rank, at most once a frame for drawing. Remote signed HP is unavailable, so the server's percentage supplies that gate. Visibility is independent of material opacity. Unit and mock packet tests cover ranks, buff changes, membership and reused spawn ids.

- AFK: there is no idle timer in the client. The local AFK flag changes only with the /afk command (13199 / 13200,
  `;afk on|off silent` to the chat server, OP_SpawnAppearance {me, 0x18, flag}) and OP_SpawnAppearance type 0x18 for
  our own spawn; while it is set a tell gets the away reply (1418 and the /afk message, or 1419). A replaced player
  keeps it. The client takes the zone's type 24 for us into that same flag, so the zone's auto AFK answers tells
  and /afk toggles it off.
- "You are now AFK." / "You are no longer AFK." come from EQEmu's zone, not the client (`Client::CheckAutoIdleAFK`,
  rules Character:EnableAutoAFK, SecondsBeforeAFK{Combat,NonCombat}Zone 1800 / 600 s, SecondsBeforeIdle* 1700 / 640
  s). It runs on each OP_ClientUpdate: one that finds the player where the last one left him, more than the AFK
  time after the last `ResetAFKTimer`, sets AFK; the next moved update clears it. Only OP_CastSpell, OP_ChannelMessage
  (not an away reply), OP_MoveItem, auto attack and OP_SpawnAppearance AFK reset that timer: walking does not (it
  resets only the idle state). A player who runs and clicks for half an hour without casting, talking or moving an
  item goes AFK at his next stop (seen live: "You are now AFK." then "You are no longer AFK." while playing). There
  is no heartbeat: a player standing still with no input sends no updates, so the zone only notices at the next
  update.
- Enhancement (`game::AfkKeepalive`, always on): while the user gives input (a key, typing, the mouse moved,
  clicked or wheeled, a pad button or stick, a touch, or any of them held; `InputState::user_activity`), the client

## Cadences (the periodic update)

The periodic update drives the timed mechanics. It and UseSkill send uninitialised stack bytes to the server (OP
0x31e6, OP 0x52fa), so they are not covered by vectors. Its schedule:

- every frame: process_all_stats;
- more than 1 s (the spawn's +0x2d8, set to now at the first frame): for a living player the endurance upkeep
  (ApplyEnduranceUpkeep @0x448bb0: the buffs' cached upkeep off the stored endurance; below 0 it becomes 0 and the
  discipline in the last buff slot goes, told to the server with OP_Buff), then the SPA 0x63 movement rate, air
  supply and environment damage (other modules);
- more than 6 s since the last tick (one timer per character at spawn+0x24c; packets do not restart it):
  positive drunkenness first decreases by one, even without BaseData. Its new value updates the body's lateral
  sway; at 70 or above it selects first person and widens the FOV, and at the alcohol-tolerance mount threshold
  it fades the mount effect and dismounts. Then
  ProcessAffects (`game::six_second_tick`, game/passage_of_time.h, over the ported `mechanics::process_affects`):
  every buff of type 2 and up whose duration was already 0 (or -1 without the permanent formula 50) is removed
  locally (no packet), every positive duration counts down by 1, and its slots apply their per-tick effects: HP
  (CalcTickHPChange: the caster's distance, the bard modifier, an inverted buff negated, a player caster's PvP loss
  75% / 70%; summed, then ModifyCurHP), mana (SPA 15 / 0x1a1, not for classes 1, 7, 8, 9, 16) and endurance (SPA
  189), a draining beneficial buff the pool cannot pay removed (OP_Buff), the mount summon, the fading lines 0x30c2
  / 0x30c3, the cast interrupt chance; a tick's HP loss breaks the first mesmerize (SPA 31) buff (OP_Buff) and ends
  the stun. Then, for a living player below its maximum HP, the HP regen (GetHPRegen; "You are bleeding to death!"
  0x318d when its flag says so; ModifyCurHP), and dead or alive the endurance regen onto the stored endurance
  (Cur_Endurance(1) clamps it) and ApplyManaRegenTick (the mana regen when Cur_Mana is below the maximum). A
  countdown is held while any buff has SPA 0x1b4 before its first blank slot (GetPCSpellAffect), or
  (Spell_IsBuffCountdownHeld @0x4dcf20) for a suspendable spell (column 200, +0x279) without SPA 0x1b4 while the
  buff timers are on hold (the spawn's +0x22d: set by a SPA 0x1b4 buff landing or ticking and by
  OP_SpawnAppearance 38 with a non-zero value, cleared by 38 with 0 and with a new spawn at zone-in); a held buff
  costs no endurance upkeep. The server's own HP, mana and endurance updates overwrite the pools whenever they come;
  the ticks move them on in between, as in the original. Without the game folder's BaseData.txt row for the
  character the ticks wait (nothing to compute the regen or the maxima with);
- more than 18 s: the profile+0x3408 countdown;
- more than 60 s: stats marked dirty.

The live sheet receives actual running and drunkenness state. Running requires nonzero forward run speed and
does not apply to a mounted rider. Damage below 1 HP can set the owner status to unconscious (3), stop movement,
lie down and select the overhead camera; its six-second HP tick bleeds and prints the bleeding message. Positive
HP recovers consciousness on the tick, stands and restores the camera. Server death remains authoritative.
SPA 343's flag-1 interruption stops any local cast without an extra interrupt packet, including the bard restart
path. StopSpellCast (stop_spell_cast.cpp:84-101): a bard's non-song only resets the cast (kept outright with
recovery at most 100 ms when it is not an item cast), with no interrupt packet and no twist restart for any reason;
anything else sends the empty OP_ManaChange for reason 0 only (feign death's StopCastAndFeignDeath, /stopsong) and
resets the cast; a bard song then also restarts the twist for a reason other than 0. See `tests/passage_of_time_test.cpp`, `tests/ability_commands_test.cpp` and `tests/movement_test.cpp`.

SPA 67 acquires an Eye summon named from the first 24 bytes of the player's name. View and control are distinct
from the player's body and mount: the Eye sends its own spawn ID's movement while the body retains gravity, air
and environment damage. Fade, despawn, death and zoning restore control and retire the remote view. Once held,
control stays while that spawn lives under the Eye's name; another matching Eye appearing later does not retire it.
Deliberate safety differences: ambiguous matching Eye names refuse acquisition rather than using unordered-map
iteration to choose one, and the name must not go on with a letter after the player's name (EQEmu names the Eye
`Eye_of_<name>` plus MakeNameUnique's three digits, so `Eye_of_Bobby000` is not Bob's; the original takes any
partial match). The packet and movement lifecycle is covered by `tests/eye_control_test.cpp`.

The swing timers (melee.h; `ActionsUi::melee_timers` each frame before the movement update): at its start, while
auto attack is on and the primary flag is still set, both flags clear and the primary, secondary and ranged timers
restart at now; a player not standing (StandState 100) has auto attack turned off; then the primary flag is set when
now - primary timer reaches the attack speed modification of (the slot-13 weapon's delay x 100, or GetReuseTime(28)
for the fist; the ranged delay after a ranged attack) and the secondary flag when now - secondary timer reaches the
slot-14 delay. The control processing (`controls.h`, inside the movement update) clears a set flag and asks for an
attack in slot 13 / 14, which the app runs (`ActionsUi::melee_attack`): the slot's item when it is a weapon the
player can use, else the fist (the profile's +0x346c damage or the skill base damage for skill 28, written into the
fist's definition); nothing to swing (true) unless it is a weapon, type 0xd or 0x16; the delay is Delay x 100, or for
a hand-to-hand item the hand-to-hand delay (the profile's +0x3470 for the fist when above 0), through the attack
speed modification; too early (elapsed below the delay, no target with +0x251, no NPC in its attack, no special
melee) returns 1 without a swing; otherwise the timer becomes last + delay when delay <= elapsed < 2 x delay, else
now, before the stand (12369 / 12368), range (124), facing (108), line of sight (123) and BodyWet (123) refusals.
The profile's +0x346c / +0x3470 are the PlayerProfile's dwords before the endurance (EQEmu sends 0). The BodyWet
compared there and in the attack rules is each spawn's (`Spawn::body_wet`): the player's from its movement, the
others' probed with each step (`WorldState::smooth`).
The swing is the combat ability attack (13); the double attack check must return exactly 1 for a second one. Slot 14
swings only when the dual wield check returns 1, and double attacks with Skill[20] above 149 or SPA 225. Slot 100 (a
combat skill, UseSkill's) sets the fist from the skill and runs on the skill's own timer (the combat skill timer
index); a player's Backstab (8) needs a piercer (type 2) in the primary, else "You need a piercing weapon in your
primary hand" (12874) and nothing is sent. A server extension's `backstab_either_hand` switch also takes one in the
secondary, a deliberate difference for a server whose backstab uses the better hand; slot 101 strikes with the primary or the fist. Slot 11 (the Range hot button) runs the ranged attack: ammo
handling picks a bow's arrows (1), a thrown stack (2) or the range item itself (0), "You have run out of ammo!!"
(12875) otherwise, and the combat ability attack sends OP_CombatAbility {target, 11, the item's skill: archery 7 for
arrows, throwing 51}; EQEmu shoots and sends the missile (`game/missiles.h`). The player is not turned toward the
target: SendCombatAbilityAttack's AimAtTarget(target, 0) @0x4fb42f reaches AimAtLocation @0x59e6c0, which writes
the shooter's heading (and the missile's angles) only when given a missile, and the missile constructor's own
AimAtTarget @0x500d79 is on the path of a missile the client makes itself, not of one from the server's packet.

The attack animations: the combat ability attack computes the attack animation choice and drops it, so no swing plays
from the client's own attack; the server's OP_Animation does it, for the attacker too (EQEmu's DoAnim reaches every
client close by, the sender included; checked live). The handler (case 0x7177) plays the action at speed / 10 on any
spawn. The client chooses only a missile source's launch animation (the attack animation choice with slot 7: C09 /
BATK for arrows) and the target's hit animation (`game::hit_animation`: D02 on a hit for damage, a spell's
TargetAnim, D01 / D03 / D04 for the environmental types).

A proc's cast animation (a deliberate difference, Hailstone's option `cast_animation_fallback`: hailstone.ini
`game.cast_animation_fallback`, Options > General "Proc Cast Animations", on by default): the cast stage Hailstone
adds for a spell that lands with no OP_BeginCast (docs/graphics.md) plays the spell's casting animation on its source,
and the local player's is sent to the zone as an OP_Animation. For a spell whose casting animation is 0 it plays 43
(CastHeals) for a beneficial spell (spell type above 0) and 44 (CastNuke) for a detrimental one, locally and in
the OP_Animation alike (`game::spell_cast_animation`). Off, such a spell plays no animation, as before. The original
plays no cast stage here at all, and what its cast animation is for a spell without one is not confirmed
(docs/unknowns.md).

Missiles (`game/missiles.h`): an item missile with a target does not sweep the zone; spell missiles sweep and
also check the target segment. Source despawn ends its missiles; target despawn removes homing. Item type 9 needs
a loaded spell id in 1..45000. Duration uses normalized AE duration (capped at 14 s except first-slot SPA 76),
otherwise 30 s for travel type 3 or 50 ms. Area target types 8, 21, 22, 23, 28 and 45 stay at the target or
destination and play stage 2; other spell missiles launch stage 1. SPA 76 area contact checks moving targets every
2 s inside 2.5 times AE range with sight; other local contact attempts use a 3 s gate and source sight, respecting
NPCNoLoS. Contact emits presentation messages, never authoritative damage. Spell 54 plays its destruction impact
one velocity step behind, at that point in the world with the source as caster and no target (EQMissile_UpdateAll
@0x501415), so it outlives the missile. Spell 859 can run a local hit presentation inside a strict 40-unit
horizontal radius, at the local player's level (@0x501342); it sends no packet, applies no successful-landing flag,
grants no buff and does not end the source's cast.

Large-source launch placement uses the renderer's actual posed role-0/name anchor. Missing authored or posed data
keeps the packet origin rather than inventing an attachment. Missile pulse timing uses a source's completed
physics step, not its last packet arrival: a non-area launch moves (now - that step) x 0.02 velocity steps further.
Hailstone: a step time not before now counts as none, and the age stops at 12 s (when a remote spawn stops
stepping), so a stale or wrapped time cannot throw the missile across the zone. Spawns start with an absent clock; local movement, fresh remote and
boat/rider steps publish StepClock time + 1. The app explicitly bridges that relative time to the missile's
32-bit steady clock. Empty step batches and stale spawns do not advance it. Fixtures cover the clock and actual
packet-to-emitter flow; recorded native vectors and live acceptance remain pending.

Environmental hits (types 0xf3..0xfd: mind burns, rotting, chilled, freezing, tormented, burned, thorns, burning,
drowning, falling, a trap; `game::environment_hit`): the player's own fall, drowning or lava damage goes through
ApplyDamage's hit report: OP_EnvDamage, then OP_Damage {the player, no attacker, the type, spell -1, the damage}
unless the HP reached -10 - SPA 0x148, and ReportSuccessfulHit on it. EQEmu passes that OP_Damage on to the clients
close by (to the sender too, but for a fall), so another's environmental hit arrives as OP_Damage. ReportSuccessfulHit
prints "YOU are burning!" (colour 0x10a) or "Bob is burning." (0x149 for 0xf3..0xfa, hidden by chat filter 0x11 at
mode 1; 0x117 for the rest) when the damage is not 0, plays fall_hit (0x73) for a fall and a crushing trap, the
victim's Hit1..4 sound (no FlshHit) and D01 (0xf3..0xf9), D04 (0xfa, 0xfb) or D03 (0xfc, 0xfd).

ReportSuccessfulHit's shared tail (@0x5311ee, `game::hit_landed`) runs after every branch. When the target is not a
corpse and the hit landed (damage above 0: a melee hit, any type above 231, a spell only when detrimental), on the
local player it closes the spell book (its OnShow stands a player the book sat down), stands a kneeling (105) or
sitting player and, while mounted, sets standing too; then the target's Hit1..4 sound plays (the spell branch's
here; the melee branch adds FlshHit 0x93 / 0x94 before it). Handle_OP_Damage itself, for a hit on the player by
another, closes the book for any damage above 0 (a beneficial spell's too), takes melee and environmental damage
off the HP, and heals a spell's negative damage. The tail's blood spurt (`PlayerClient_SpawnBloodSpurt` @0x58f1c0)
goes with the player's own landed hit of skill 0..3, 8, 10, 36 or 77 when the target has its exact authored blood
attachment and the player's sprite can be loaded (docs/formats/particles.md).

## Animations

`game/spawn_animation.*` runs two animation objects per spawn (`render/actor_renderer.cpp`). Classic (WLD) models
take old-style numbers (C01..T09): the old-style movement and idle choice, the sitting substitutions, the
replacement for a clip the model lacks, and a 100 ms cross-fade (both layers start with 100.0, whatever the
animation). New-style models (EQG races) take sequence codes 0x4f..0xb6 (an old-style number goes through the
old-to-new map): the movement animation selection while moving, the idle choice while still, and the track search:
the EQG model's tracks are the `<tag>_<style>_<n>` animations the animation sound tracks bind (styles BA 1H 2H DW ST
MT, variants 1..4; other names are not loaded), a sequence the model lacks falls back through the table (BLSC ->
SLSC -> SLPR -> STND), the weapon style is the column picked from the player-state weapon bits, STND / IDLE become
STDA / IDLA while aggressive or after an attack, and the variant is rand() % the variants the column has. The
sequence found is the animation playing, and the cross-fade is (new.blendIn + current.blendOut) / 2 from the table.
EQG models also answer the classic codes, each old-style number's sequence in the base style, variant 1
(`assets/eqg_character.h` classic_aliases).

**Bone groups** (`game/anim_layers.*`, `render/actor_layers.*`). Each actor splits its bones into two CBoneGroups when
its animations are set up (0x4090f0 / 0x409260 by HasNewStyleHierarchicalModel; EnumerateBones @0x10040f80
puts each bone in the group its tag's callback answers): group 0 the upper body (classic tags whose part after the
model code starts FA HE FI FO BI NE HI CH, but CHCHEST_; EQG tags CHEST* but CHEST_CHEST01, HEAD*, ARM*, NECK*),
group 1 the rest (root, pelvis, legs, and the points the callbacks do not name, such as R_POINT). Each group
cross-fades on its own, linear over the blend time (BlendToAnimation @0x1003fcc0; asked for the animation it plays,
it does nothing). Which group plays what:

- Classic models (0x4b15b0): the animation asked goes to group 0, the "queued" one to group 1; they are the same
  but for a rider (the rider animation 0x4a / 0x4e queued on the legs, the action on the arms) and a moving (+0x7c not
  0) humanoid (PlayerBase::HasProperty(1), body type 1) starting an action of the table at 0xdced38 (C02..C09 and
  0x27..0x2c, the instruments and casting): nothing is queued and the legs keep moving. While group 0 has not played
  such an action through (HasCompletedCycle), a humanoid's walk or run (table 0xdced88) starts on the legs alone and
  other requests (standing) wait. When the action ends, group 0 cross-fades back (100 ms) to what the legs play and
  takes their position (SyncToBoneGroup).
- New-style models (0x58afa0): a sequence of layer kind 1 (attacks, casting) plays on group 0 alone while group 1
  plays a kind 2 sequence (walk, run, turn), or for a rider (which plays nothing else); a walk / run / turn then goes
  to the legs while the unfinished action keeps the upper body (0x5890a0, 0x58ac90's modes); another
  sequence (stand, idle) starts on both groups when either group's mode is 2, cutting the action (0x589410).
  Each group's cross-fade is (new.blendIn + that group's old blendOut) / 2.
- New-style riders select movement and idle sequences from mounted style column 5, using the mount's motion.
  The layer groups retain the requested style column as part of sequence identity, so the same sequence in a
  different column can start without restarting a repeated request for the same column.
- A request that finds the upper body busy (`render/actor_layers.cpp` start_action). Classic: an OP_Animation reaches
  0x4b15b0 through the object's slot 3 with restart 1 and key 1, and 0x4b0900 decides whether it may start at
  once (`game::classic_may_interrupt`: the blend and rider animations, D05, 0x21 and 0x2c always; never over itself;
  else only over an animation of table 0xdcece8). Refused while group 0 has not played its cycle through
  (HasCompletedCycle, slot 0x1c; slot 0x24 is the group's queued animation +0xc, which nothing sets here), the
  actions 1..0xb and 0x2d..0x2f become the follow-up +0x10c4 (the last one asked wins) and the movement ones
  (0x11..0x19, 0x21, 0x24, 0x4b, 0x4c) reset it to 0x20; the next stand request (P01) once the upper body is free
  plays the follow-up instead. New-style: 0x58afa0 takes each group's 0x58ac90 mode; mode 1 records the
  sequence at +0x10c4 / +0x10cc, which nothing reads back to play (only copied and reset), so it is dropped; mode 3
  (the same sequence and column) keeps what plays; 0x588e20 (the upper body alone) also plays nothing while
  group 0's sequence is of layer kind 0 and there is no mount answer.
- The same animation asked again: BlendToAnimation leaves a group that already plays it alone, so neither object
  replays an action that is still the group's animation (classic: it follows itself as the follow-up when asked
  before its cycle is through, which then starts nothing new either).
- Classic P08 / O02 (0x4b15b0's idle block with 0x4b0860, read from the exe:
  `game::classic_plain_stand_after`). A stand request (P01, or any animation the model lacks even after 0x4b1210's
  replacement) plays the follow-up +0x10c4 when there is one; otherwise P01 when the animation playing (+0x10c0) is
  P01, C01..D05 (1..0x10), L01 (0x11), P03 (0x22), T04..T09 (0x2a..0x2f), L11 (0x4b) or none (0xfe, EQPlayer's
  constructor value), and P08 (0x47) after anything else: the run, a jump or fall, a duck, a posture change, the
  instruments, a social, P08 itself. An idle request (O01) after such an animation is O02 (0x48). A model without
  P08 / O02 plays P01 / O01. When the stand request plays the follow-up, +0x10c4 becomes 0x20 after a named
  animation, else 0x47, so the next stand is P08 (a follow-up of 0x47 plays P08 and is reset the same way). The
  layers keep an OP_Animation action as +0x10c0 until it ends (0x4b15b0 writes +0x10c0 only for what it
  starts), unless a walk or run took the legs under it; so after a social the stand is P08, after an attack P01.
- Ducking (PlayerZoneClient::ChangePosition @0x5a0bc0): a change to 'o' (111) plays the duck 0x18 (L08; CRCH on
  a new-style model) once, unless it or the duck walk 0x16 (L06 / CWLK) is playing; 'd' (100) from 'o' plays it
  backward. A plain play, not a held posture: a walk or a jump replaces it, and a still ducking spawn asks for
  nothing more, so the crouch shows without moving (`game::choose_spawn_animation`).
- Climbing: CollidingType (+0x24, 6 on a ladder) reaches 0x4afe60 / SelectMovementAnim, which plays L07 / CLMB
  forward going up and reversed going down. Fresh moving remote spawns probe the same world sweep for ladder and
  switch contacts; a stationary spawn retains its contact, while riders, corpses and NoCollide actors do not probe.
  The probe's world follows the player's passable-ladder rule (`ZoneMoveWorld::apply_remote_rules`), so other
  spawns climb Cabilis's passable ladders when the player would; the open doors the player's /nav or /stick passes
  are the player's own, and other spawns' probes meet every door. Hailstone, for the frame's
  cost: only spawns within the actor clip of the player are probed (`WorldState::remote_probe_range`, the camera's
  `game::actor_clip_distance`, 550 at the default ActorClipPlane); a farther one is not drawn and has no contact.
  Only a ladder gives a contact the animation shows, so the probe sweeps the doors only after a ladder hit (a door
  hit then gives the switch type over it, as in the full sweep), and not the vehicles or NPC bodies.
  This publishes animation contact state; full remote collision correction and the physical-radius gap remain open.
- Other spawns' FeetWet / BodyWet (+0xa1 / +0xa2) are probed with each step (`WorldState::smooth`: CollisionPass's
  sub_00506d20 at the spawn's z and CheckRegionChange at the feet + 0.25), so they swim and tread water.

## Inputs the client cannot provide yet

- An item's current power: only its maximum comes, so the item display's power line shows the maximum against itself
  (100%). A deliberate difference there: the original's `current * 100 / max` is an int32 product that wraps past
  21.4 million; with the maximum standing in for the current power the wrap would only make the 100% meaningless, so
  the client multiplies in 64 bits.
- Leadership bonuses, the server's PvP ruleset, the server's regen rule for `IsRestingForFastRegen`, the 21 extra
  item lookups (tribute-like containers), the caster level of buffs that arrived without one (OP_BuffCreate; the
  player's level is used).

The sheet's stored HP (profile +0x3394 BaseHP, which current HP adds the HP bonus to) is the server's current HP
(`LiveState::hp`), as OP_HPUpdate's value is stored there for the local player. Left at 0, current HP read 0 and the
weight calculation's low-health slowdown applied: AGI halved (a level-60 cleric's max endurance 1614 for the
server's 1668) and the movement frame's weight factor 0.5.

The player's own HP is OP_HPUpdate's points plus the item sum (`DerivedStats::hp_bonus`), as Cur_HP and Max_HP add
it: EQEmu sends the player its own HP without the items' HP. A server extension's vitals (`CharacterVitals`) are the
whole value and get nothing added, and nothing is added to the current HP while the player is dead. The app tells
the connection the item sum each in-zone frame (app/stats_ui.cpp `share_own_hp_items`; kept through the camp
countdown, gone out of the zone), and everything that shows, reports or tests the player's current HP reads
`net::GameConnection::own_hp`: the windows (labels 17, 18, 19 and 70, gauge 1, the ability book's HP percent, the
developer group window's own row), the player's own spawn's HP percent (SetSpawnHP's, so `Spawn[...].PctHPs`, a self
target and a plugin's spawn), `Me.CurrentHPs`, `Me.MaxHPs` and `Me.PctHPs`, `hailstone.player()`'s `cur_hp` /
`max_hp`, a plugin's `HailstonePlayer`, the knocked-out test (`player_down`: the mouse and the context menu, OP_Save),
UseSkill's Cur_HP check, the fall damage report's knock-out floor, the caster's Max_HP in CastSpell and the hit
sound's HP. The bare packet value goes below 1 while a geared player still stands, which is why none of these read
it. The developer stats window compares its computed Max_HP with that sum. The regeneration ticks and the sheet's
stored HP keep the packet's value (Cur_HP adds the item sum to it).

## Fellowships

Fellowship state comes from the server. Create, invitations, MOTD, leadership, removal, leaving, disbanding,
XP sharing and camp placement/destruction send native requests and wait for the server's next roster. Stock
EQEmu does not send RoF2 fellowship packets, so against it the window stays empty. The
first roster member leads; members may toggle their own sharing, and the leader may toggle another member's.
Confirmation dialogs recheck fellowship identity and permission before sending. An invitation shows its dialog
only when the inviter's spawn is known, as `Handle_Opcode_0x62ab` @0x667db0 does; yes sends action 3 and no
action 4 (`Send_Opcode_0x7121` @0x668710). `/fsay` uses channel 21 and requires membership (`FellowshipSay`
@0x524330: eqstr 4573 in colour 342 otherwise). MQ's `fellowship` spawn filter matches the received roster by name.
The window opens with the native Ctrl+Shift+F default or the EQ menu's Fellowship entry; both use the same
window-toggle dispatch, and the shortcut can be changed in Options > Keys.

Both UI packs show sortable members, online/last-seen information, sharing/capped state, a 1023-byte MOTD draft,
exact normal/AA vitality pools and caps, server-supplied camp kits and the active camp's location and remaining
duration. The inventory also shows normal/AA vitality bars through native gauge types 147/148 (AA from level 51).
Their truncated pool/cap ratio follows the original; zero caps draw empty and the computation cannot overflow a
64-bit pool. Both roster views identify the leader. The server
enforces all admission, XP, supplies, placement, cooldown and expiration rules. Zoning retains membership while
clearing pending invitations and kit queries; disconnecting clears the whole state.

**Extension beyond RoF2:** the client supports up to 64 members instead of RoF2's twelve. This is storage and
presentation capacity, not an entitlement to join: twelve-slot messages are read exactly as RoF2 reads them, and
expanded server messages must follow the array layout in `docs/protocol/rof2-packets.md`. EQEmu PR #5134
